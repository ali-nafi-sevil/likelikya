The Lycian Way

Likya Yolu — ~540 km, Fethiye to Antalya
What it is. A 540-kilometre waymarked long-distance footpath along the southwest coast of Turkey, opened in 1999 by Kate Clow. It links the major Lycian sites, follows older goat paths, Roman roads, and Ottoman muleteer tracks, and ranges from sea level to about 1800 m.

Route at a glance

The trail begins at Ovacık, just south of Fethiye, and ends — officially — at Geyikbayırı outside Antalya. Most walkers do it east–west or west–east in sections rather than end-to-end. The full trail takes around 30 days.

Highlights, west to east

  • Ovacık → Faralya: the dramatic descent into Butterfly Valley.
  • Kabak → Sıdyma → Pydnai: remote stretch through wooded ridges.
  • Patara → Kalkan → Kaş: Mediterranean coast walking with frequent swimming.
  • Kaş → Kekova → Demre: arguably the prettiest section of all — Aperlai, Apollonia, the sunken city.
  • Demre → Finike → Adrasan: long beach days and the climb over Musa Dağı.
  • Olympos → Çıralı → Tahtalı: Chimaera flames, the cable car to 2365 m.
  • Phaselis → Göynük → Geyikbayırı: closing stretch into the Antalya basin.

Practical

The official guidebook is Kate Clow's The Lycian Way, regularly updated and accompanied by GPS files. Most sections are well-marked with red-and-white blazes. Water sources are reliable but spaced; carry at least 2 litres.

Best season

Late February to early May and September to mid-November. Summer is too hot in most sections. Winter is walkable in the lowlands but mountain crossings (Tahtalı, Olympos high route) can have snow.

Doing it slowly

The Lycian Way rewards stopping. The ancient cities along the route are not just waymarks — each one is a small lesson in how a city worked in the bronze, classical, or Roman period, and most are quiet enough to read in peace. Plan rest days at Kaş, Çıralı, and Olympos at minimum.
