Myra

Μύρα — modern Demre, Antalya province
At a glance. One of the six leading cities of the Lycian League (three votes), with one of the most photographed rock-tomb cliffs in Lycia and a Roman theatre big enough for 11,000 spectators. Modern Demre sits on top of the ancient city; the church of Saint Nicholas stands just to the north.

The rock tombs

Cut into the sheer cliff above the theatre, the Myra necropolis is the densest concentration of monumental Lycian house tombs anywhere. Many were originally painted; faint pigment traces survive in a few. The facades imitate timber houses, with carved doorways and porches.

The theatre and city

The Roman theatre below is the largest in Lycia, with a stage building partially standing and a long inscription naming its sponsors. The rest of ancient Myra is buried under modern greenhouses and Demre's town sprawl — the harbour at Andriake a few kilometres away is the city's main surviving secondary site.

Saint Nicholas

Nicholas was bishop of Myra in the 4th century AD. His relics were stolen by Italian sailors in 1087 and taken to Bari, but his original church in Demre — a much-rebuilt Byzantine basilica — is a major Orthodox pilgrimage site. The Father Christmas legend descends, by a long route, from his historical reputation for secret gift-giving.
