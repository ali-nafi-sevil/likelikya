Letoon

Λητῷον — near Kumluova, 4 km south of Xanthos, Muğla province
At a glance. The federal sanctuary of the Lycian League, dedicated to Leto and her children Apollo and Artemis. UNESCO World Heritage Site since 1988, jointly with neighbouring Xanthos (4 km north).

What it was

Letoon was the religious centre of the Lycian League — the place where the federation's twenty-three member cities met for major festivals and where treaties and important political decisions were ratified. It sits on a flat alluvial plain near the mouth of the Xanthos River, where a natural spring fed both the sanctuary's pools and the goddess's cult.

What to see

  • Three temples, side by side, dedicated to Leto, Apollo, and Artemis respectively. The Leto temple is the largest; the Artemis temple has unusual rock-cut elements.
  • The Hellenistic theatre, almost complete, with arched passageways still standing.
  • The Nymphaeum — a large semicircular fountain still partly flooded by the sacred spring. Frogs and terrapins live in it.
  • The Trilingual Stele (found in 1973) — a 4th-century BC inscription in Lycian, Greek, and Aramaic. It was the Rosetta Stone of the Lycian language, allowing modern scholars to read Lycian texts for the first time. The original is in the Fethiye Museum; a copy is on site.
  • A Byzantine basilica overlaid on the sanctuary, built when the cult was finally closed in late antiquity.

The Leto myth, localised

According to local legend, after giving birth to Apollo and Artemis on Delos, Leto fled to Lycia and stopped at this very spring to bathe her children. Local shepherds tried to stop her; she turned them into frogs — which is why the nymphaeum is still full of them. The story is Ovid's, but the Lycians clearly believed some version of it long before.

Practical

Getting there. Just south of Kumluova village, signposted from the Patara–Xanthos road. 10 minutes by car from Xanthos.
Best time. Early morning or late afternoon. Midday in summer is brutal on the open plain.
Combine with. Xanthos (4 km north) and Patara (15 km south) — easily all three in one day.
