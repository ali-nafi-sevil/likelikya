Phellos

Φελλός — on the ridge above Çukurbağ, Antalya province
At a glance. The Archaic mother city of Antiphellos (modern Kaş), sitting at 850 m on a limestone ridge above the village of Çukurbağ. Older than its more famous daughter on the coast, and reached today only on foot — a 1–2 hour walk up through pine and maquis.

The older twin

Phellos is the older of the two cities — attested already in the 6th century BC and minting its own coinage with a boar's head in the 5th. The name comes from the Greek for "stony ground," which is exactly what the ridge is. As Mediterranean trade increased, the high city needed a proper harbour: Antiphellos down on the coast grew up as that harbour, and eventually overtook the mother city in wealth and political importance.

What's still there

  • A small theatre, partly cut into the ridge rock.
  • A Hellenistic temple, foundations only.
  • Cisterns for water storage — the ridge has no spring.
  • Fortification walls running around the natural acropolis.
  • The "house tomb" — a free-standing rock carved into the shape of a Lycian house tomb, with a bull-relief on one side.
  • A scatter of sarcophagi along the approach path.

The walk up

The route starts at the upper edge of Çukurbağ village (about 8 km inland from Kaş) and climbs through fragrant maquis to the ridge. The walk itself is the best way to understand the original geography of the twin cities — a defensible high city that needed a sea outlet, and eventually grew one. Once on the ridge the views run from the Akdağlar behind to the open Mediterranean ahead, with Meis on the horizon.

Practical

Getting there. Drive or dolmuş from Kaş to Çukurbağ (~8 km). The trailhead is at the upper end of the village; locals can point you up. Allow 3–4 hours round trip, longer if you linger.
Bring. Water, sturdy shoes, sun cover. No facilities on the ridge.
Combine with. A morning at Phellos, an afternoon swim and the theatre at Antiphellos — the twin cities in one day.
