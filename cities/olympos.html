Olympos

Ὄλυμπος — between Çıralı and Adrasan
At a glance. A Lycian and later Roman city tucked into a wooded ravine where a clear river meets a pebble beach. Held three votes in the Lycian League. Captured at one point by the pirate Zeniketos before being cleared by the Roman general P. Servilius Vatia.

The setting

Olympos is the most romantic-looking ruin in Lycia. The river runs through the middle of the site; bay laurel, oleander, and plane trees crowd the walls; sarcophagi sit half-swallowed by undergrowth on both banks. You enter the site by wading or stepping along the riverbed.

What to see

  • Necropolis on both river banks, with several inscribed sarcophagi.
  • Roman bathhouse with surviving floor mosaics.
  • Bishop's palace from the Byzantine period.
  • Harbour and acropolis at the river mouth.
  • The Chimaera — a 30-minute walk up the mountain behind: methane vents in the limestone that have burned continuously for at least 2,500 years, identified in antiquity with the fire-breathing Chimera defeated by Bellerophon.

Modern Çıralı & Olympos

The valley is split between two villages. Olympos, on the inland side, is backpacker treehouse country. Çıralı, on the beach side, is quieter and family-oriented. Both are kept low-rise by loggerhead nesting regulation, which has saved this stretch of coast from concrete.
