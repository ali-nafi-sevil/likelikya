Patara

Πάταρα · Pttara — modern Gelemiş, Antalya province
At a glance. Federal capital of the Lycian League, principal harbour of the region, and the birthplace of Saint Nicholas of Myra (yes — that one). 18 kilometres of nesting beach for loggerhead turtles.

Why it mattered

Patara was Lycia's largest harbour and the meeting place of its federal assembly. The recently reconstructed Bouleuterion — a 1,400-seat council chamber — was the actual legislature of the Lycian League and the closest thing the ancient world had to a representative parliament. Patara held three votes, the maximum.

What to see

  • Bouleuterion / Council Chamber — reconstructed; you can sit on the original tiers.
  • Theatre for about 5,000 spectators, with the original stage wall partly standing.
  • Triple-arched gate of Modestus, AD 100 — the main city gate.
  • Hadrian's granary on the harbour.
  • Lighthouse of Nero, the oldest dated lighthouse in the world (AD 64–65), reconstructed from fallen blocks.
  • Patara beach — 18 km of dune-backed sand, closed to humans at night during turtle nesting season.

The harbour that wasn't

The Xanthos River silted up the Patara harbour over centuries; today the ancient quays sit several hundred metres inland, behind a lagoon. The silting is one reason Patara declined in late antiquity while neighbouring coastal sites kept going.
