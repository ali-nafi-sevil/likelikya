Araxa

Ἄραξα — near Ören, Muğla province
At a glance. A small Lycian city in the upper Xanthos valley, near the modern village of Ören (Fethiye district). Best known today for a single document: the Araxa Decree, one of the most informative civic inscriptions to survive from the Lycian League period.

The Araxa Decree

Discovered in the 19th century and now studied as a key source on Lycian federal politics, the Araxa Decree is a long honorific inscription set up by the city in praise of one of its citizens, Orthagoras, listing his services to the city and to the Lycian League across his lifetime. The text gives us specific names of cities, federal offices, conflicts with neighbours (including Bubon and Termessos), and the workings of the league assembly — things we know about the Lycian federation in this kind of detail almost nowhere else.

The site

Araxa itself is one of the least-visited Lycian sites. There are no monumental theatres or temples standing — but there are walls, cisterns, rock-cut tombs in the surrounding cliffs, and visible city boundaries. The setting in the upper Xanthos valley is wide and green; in spring the fields around the site are full of wildflowers.

Why it's worth a stop

  • For anyone interested in how the Lycian League actually worked, Araxa is the place to think about — it produced a decree that's used in every modern study of the federation.
  • The site is almost always empty — a chance to walk a Lycian city in peace.
  • It pairs well with Tlos (downstream) and Pinara for a full day in the inland western valley.

Practical

Getting there. Drive from Fethiye towards Seki/Söğütlü; the site lies near Ören village. Access is on foot — ask locally for directions. There are no formal facilities.
Best time. Spring (March–May) for wildflowers and cool temperatures.
Combine with. Tlos and Pinara — a triangle of upper-valley Lycian cities in one day.
