Sagalassos

Σαγαλασσός — Ağlasun, Burdur province
At a glance. A spectacular Pisidian upland city at 1,500 m in the Taurus mountains, just north of Lycia proper. Reached by a long drive through pine forest, and worth every kilometre. Under continuous excavation by the Catholic University of Leuven since 1990 — one of the most active sites in Turkey.

The setting

Sagalassos sits in a natural bowl on the south slope of Akdağ. The view south to the Pamphylian plain is colossal. Cloud often sits halfway down the mountain at midday.

The reconstructed monuments

  • Antonine Nymphaeum — a Roman fountain monument, dismantled by earthquake and painstakingly re-erected. It flows again with mountain spring water and you can drink from it.
  • Theatre for 9,000 spectators — the highest Roman theatre in Anatolia.
  • Upper and lower agoras with colonnades partly re-erected.
  • Heroon with a frieze of dancing girls in high relief.
  • Roman baths on a colossal scale, with surviving mosaic floors.

Sagalassos and Lycia

Strictly Pisidian, not Lycian — but it sits at the very edge of the Lycian world and is so close in spirit that any trip to the region should include it. Combine with Termessos for a "mountain cities" side-trip out of Antalya.
