Andriake

Ἀνδριάκη — the harbour of Myra, modern Çayağzı
At a glance. The ancient harbour of Myra, 5 km southwest of modern Demre at the mouth of the Demre River. Home to one of the best preserved Roman granaries in the eastern Mediterranean — Hadrian's Horrea — and a small but excellent on-site museum.

The Granary of Hadrian

Built in AD 119–139 under the emperor Hadrian to supply Rome with Lycian and Anatolian grain, the granary is a long rectangular block of eight rooms, with masonry walls preserved to several metres in height. Inscriptions and a portrait bust of Hadrian still survive above the entrance. The scale gives a sense of how much grain Lycia was exporting to the Roman heartland in the 2nd century AD.

What else to see

  • The Lycian Civilisations Museum, built into the granary and surrounding excavations — well-curated, English signs, worth an hour even if you've been to bigger archaeology museums.
  • The agora and warehouses along the silted harbour.
  • A Byzantine basilica and an octagonal building of unclear function.
  • The shoreline — silted up since antiquity, but atmospheric and quiet.
