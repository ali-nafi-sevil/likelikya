Telmessos

Τελμησσός — modern Fethiye, Muğla province
At a glance. The westernmost major Lycian city, sitting in the natural amphitheatre of Fethiye Bay. Famous in antiquity for its soothsayers; famous today for the rock-cut Tomb of Amyntas looking down over the harbour.

The Tomb of Amyntas

A 4th-century BC rock-cut tomb in the style of a Greek Ionic temple, carved straight into the cliff above modern Fethiye. The façade is intact; the inscription names Amyntas son of Hermagios. Best photographed at sunset, when the limestone goes the colour of honey.

Other remains

  • Lycian sarcophagi scattered through the modern town, including a famous one near the post office.
  • Theatre partly excavated near the marina.
  • Crusader-era Fethiye castle on the hilltop above town, built on Lycian foundations.

The earthquakes

Fethiye has been heavily damaged by earthquakes more than once in living memory — 1957 levelled most of the older town, which is why the modern centre is largely post-1960. The ancient remains survived because they were rock-cut or freestanding stone; the brick-and-timber Ottoman fabric did not.
