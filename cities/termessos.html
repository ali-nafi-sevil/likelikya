Termessos

Τερμησσός — Güllük Dağı Milli Parkı, Antalya province
At a glance. A Pisidian (not strictly Lycian) mountain city at about 1,050 m on the flanks of Solymos. Famous for being the only place Alexander the Great chose not to attack — he called it the "eagle's nest."

Why it's astonishing

Termessos is the best-preserved upland city in Turkey, partly because it has never been continuously inhabited and partly because almost everything is enormous, carved from local stone, and difficult to remove. The walk in from the park gate climbs through pine forest, past Roman tomb façades, to a saddle where the city opens up: agora, gymnasium, cisterns, theatre, bouleuterion. The theatre seats 4,200 with a view straight down 1,000 m to the Pamphylian plain.

What to see

  • Theatre on the cliff edge — possibly the most dramatically sited in Anatolia.
  • Six cisterns for water storage — Termessos had no river.
  • Gymnasium & baths, still standing several storeys high.
  • Bouleuterion (council chamber) with one wall still complete.
  • Necropolis spread across a hillside — hundreds of monumental sarcophagi, some thrown open by earthquake or treasure hunters.
  • Wildlife — ibex are regularly seen on the cliffs; wolves and brown bear pass through the park.

Getting there

35 km west of Antalya, in Güllük Dağı National Park. Park at the entrance and allow at least 4 hours on foot — the climb is steep and rewards lingering.
