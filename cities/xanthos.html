Xanthos

Ξάνθος · Arñna — modern Kınık, Antalya province
At a glance. The political and ceremonial capital of ancient Lycia. Held three votes — the maximum — in the Lycian League. UNESCO World Heritage Site since 1988, jointly with the nearby sanctuary at Letoon.

Where

On a high bluff above the Eşen Çayı (the ancient Xanthos River), 8 km inland from the Mediterranean and the long beach of Patara. The site occupies two acropolises with a saddle between them.

The two defiances

Xanthos is famous for choosing self-destruction twice. The first time, around 540 BC, the Persian general Harpagus encircled the city; the Xanthians killed their own women, children, and slaves, set the acropolis on fire, and died fighting. The second time, in 42 BC, Brutus besieged the city during the Roman civil wars and the same thing happened, with the same scale. On both occasions a handful of Xanthians survived to rebuild.

What to see

  • The Harpy Tomb (about 480 BC) — a tall pillar tomb with relief panels of winged female figures carrying off children, currently represented on-site by a cast (the originals are in the British Museum).
  • The Inscribed Pillar — a 4-sided stele covered in the longest known Lycian-language inscription, plus a Greek epigram, recording the deeds of a 5th-century BC ruler.
  • The Nereid Monument — a small Ionic temple-tomb of about 390 BC, also now in the British Museum.
  • The Roman theatre and agora — built directly over the earlier Lycian structures.
  • Byzantine basilica with fragmentary mosaic floors.

The Letoon, 4 km away

The federal sanctuary of the Lycian League, dedicated to Leto and her children Apollo and Artemis. Three temples, a nymphaeum, and the famous trilingual stele (Lycian, Greek, Aramaic) that helped decipher the Lycian language. Visit together with Xanthos.
