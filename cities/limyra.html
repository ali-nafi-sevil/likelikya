Limyra

Λίμυρα — near Turunçova, east of Finike, Antalya province
At a glance. The political centre of eastern Lycia in the 4th century BC, capital of the Lycian dynast Pericle, with one of the most unusual heroon-tombs in the ancient world and a sprawling site under continuous Austrian excavation since the 1960s.

The dynast Pericle

In the early 4th century BC, Lycia was effectively unified for the first time under the dynast Pericle of Limyra, who pulled the eastern cities away from the older western centre at Xanthos. His tomb sits on the acropolis above the modern site — a small temple-tomb in the Greek style with caryatids in place of columns, very rare in this region.

What to see

  • The Heroon of Pericle on the acropolis — climb up for the view as much as the building.
  • Cenotaph of Gaius Caesar — built for Augustus's grandson, who died at Limyra in AD 4 returning from the east. Few such monuments survive.
  • Roman theatre, partly excavated.
  • Hundreds of sarcophagi scattered across the necropolises east and west of the city.
  • Byzantine basilica.
  • The river running through the site — full of terrapins, an unexpected feature.
