Arykanda

Ἀρύκανδα — Arif village, near Finike, Antalya province
At a glance. A spectacular inland Lycian city terraced down a steep mountainside at around 700 m, sometimes called "the Delphi of Lycia" for the dramatic vertical layout. Inhabited from at least the 5th century BC into Byzantine times.

The terraced city

Arykanda is one of those Anatolian sites where the setting is half the point. The whole city is stacked down the south flank of a mountain in a series of terraces, each one a complete civic complex: agora at one level, gymnasium below, theatre below that, bath complex further down still. The walk through it is essentially a descent.

What to see

  • The theatre with view straight down the valley.
  • The stadium — narrow, built on a terrace just above the theatre, an unusual layout.
  • The agora and bouleuterion.
  • The bath-gymnasium complex, well preserved with vaulted ceilings still in place.
  • The Trajaneum — temple of Trajan — on the upper terrace, with its substructure largely intact.
  • The necropolis on the slopes above the city.

Reputation in antiquity

Arrian and other ancient writers note that the Arykandans had a reputation for being more interested in feasting than in politics. The city was reasonably wealthy through the Roman period; coins minted here are still common finds.
