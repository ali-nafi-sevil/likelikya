The Kekova area

Simena · Aperlai · Teimioussa · Andriake
At a glance. A sheltered strait between Kekova island and the Lycian mainland, ringed by four ancient cities and one half-sunken one. The whole area is part of the Kaş–Kekova Special Environmental Protection Area; swimming and snorkelling are restricted directly over the sunken ruins.

Simena (Kaleköy)

A tiny village still inside its ancient walls, climbing up to a Byzantine castle topped with a Lycian-period rock-cut theatre of seven rows — possibly the smallest ancient theatre anywhere. Sarcophagi lie scattered through and around the village. The castle view across the strait to the sunken city is one of the defining images of Lycia.

Teimioussa (Üçağız)

The mainland anchorage opposite Simena. Modern Üçağız is a small fishing village built among the ancient harbour quays, sarcophagi, and tombs. The name "Üçağız" — "three mouths" — refers to the three entrances to the sheltered bay.

Aperlai

Reached by boat or on foot via the Lycian Way. A small Lycian city whose walls run partly into the sea — sea level has risen ~2 m since antiquity, and the lower courses of the city are now submerged. Underwater archaeology has documented harbour walls, baths, and a purple-dye workshop using murex shells.

The sunken city

Along the north shore of Kekova island, stairways, walls, basins, and cisterns of an early Byzantine settlement run straight into the water and continue below the surface — the result of a major earthquake in the 2nd century AD. Swimming directly over the ruins is banned, but glass-bottom boats and kayaks cross the bay continuously.

Andriake

Just to the east, the harbour of ancient Myra — with one of the best preserved Roman granaries in the eastern Mediterranean (the Horrea of Hadrian), built in the 2nd century AD to supply Rome with Lycian grain.
