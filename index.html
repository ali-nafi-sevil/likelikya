Land of sun, sea, and stone

What is this place

A small guide to a vast landscape

✦

likelikya is a working guide to Lycia — the southwestern corner of Anatolia that the Greeks, Persians, Romans, and a stubborn local people all called home in turn. The site collects what we can know about each ancient city, the trail that now strings them together, the wild ecology that has reclaimed the ruins, and the modern towns that live on top of them. It is built slowly, by hand, and will grow.

The Lycian Way

540 kilometres of waymarked coast

From Fethiye to Antalya, the Lycian Way is one of the world's great long-distance walks. It traces goat paths and Roman roads along the cliffs, dropping into ancient harbours and climbing through pine forest to abandoned cities still half-buried in stone.

Living landscape

Ecology of the Lycian coast

The same limestone that built the cities still shapes the life on them. Mediterranean maquis covers the hillsides, posidonia meadows hold the seabed together, fire-adapted pine renews itself in cycles, and a handful of monk seals still slip into sea caves near Kekova.
