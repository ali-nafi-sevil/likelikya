Western Lycia

Around Fethiye and the Xanthos valley

Telmessos · modern Fethiye

Telmessos

The great rock-cut Tomb of Amyntas looks down over Fethiye harbour. The city's reputation for soothsayers reached as far as Alexander.

CoastRock tombs
 Upper Xanthos valley

Araxa

A quiet inland city famous for one of the most informative civic inscriptions of the Lycian League era — the Araxa Decree honouring Orthagoras.

InlandDecree
 Lycian League · vote of 3

Pinara

A theatre carved into a wooded mountainside, beneath a colossal sheer cliff honeycombed with empty tombs.

MountainTheatre
 "Brightest jewel of Lycia"

Tlos

Acropolis-on-acropolis, lived in continuously from Bronze Age through Ottoman times. The Bellerophon tomb is here.

MountainBellerophon
 Lycian capital · UNESCO

Xanthos

The political and ceremonial centre of the Lycian League, where the inhabitants twice chose mass suicide over surrender.

InlandPillar tombs
 Federal sanctuary · UNESCO

Letoon

The religious centre of the Lycian League, where treaties were ratified and the trilingual stele unlocked the Lycian language.

InlandThree temples
 Birthplace of Saint Nicholas

Patara

Federal capital of the Lycian League, with a 5,000-seat theatre and an 18 km beach now closed at night to loggerheads.

CoastTheatre · Lighthouse
Central Lycia

The Teke peninsula — Kaş, Kekova, Demre

Antiphellos · modern Kaş

Antiphellos

The Hellenistic harbour city below Phellos: small theatre, Lion Sarcophagus on the main street, and Meis on the horizon.

CoastTheatre · Tombs
 Mother city on the ridge

Phellos

The older twin, at 850 m on the limestone ridge above Çukurbağ — reached on foot, fewer remains, fewer visitors.

Ridge · 850 mHouse tomb
 "The dark ones"

Kyaneai

A small Lycian city with the densest concentration of sarcophagi anywhere in Lycia — they stand thick on the ridge like a stone forest.

RidgeSarcophagi
 Sunken city of Kekova

Simena · Aperlai · Teimioussa

Three Lycian ports on a sheltered strait — half-walls, harbour quays, and basins now several metres underwater.

CoastSubmerged
 Modern Demre · 3 votes

Myra

One of the leading Lycian cities, with a massive Roman theatre, the most photographed rock-tomb cliff in Lycia, and the church of Saint Nicholas next door.

CoastRock tombs
 Harbour of Myra

Andriake

Myra's port, home to Hadrian's Granary — one of the best preserved Roman granaries in the eastern Mediterranean — and the Lycian Civilisations Museum.

CoastGranary · Museum
Eastern Lycia

The Olympos coast and the bay of Antalya

Dynast Pericle's capital

Limyra

The eastern Lycian centre in the 4th century BC. Heroon of Pericle on the acropolis, cenotaph of Gaius Caesar, terrapins in the river.

InlandHeroon
 The "Delphi of Lycia"

Arykanda

A vertiginously terraced city stacked down a mountainside above the Aykırı valley. Theatre, stadium, Trajaneum — each on its own level.

MountainTerraced
 Pirate-friendly port

Olympos

A city tucked into a wooded ravine where a clear river meets the sea, ruled at one point by the pirate Zeniketos.

CoastChimaera flames
 Three harbours, one city

Phaselis

A wealthy port founded by colonists from Rhodes, with a main street running between two of its three harbours.

CoastAqueduct · Agora
Highland neighbours

Just beyond Lycia — the Pisidian cities

Not Lycian, strictly speaking, but inseparable from the same coast and trail network — the Pisidian highland cities sit on the edge of Lycia and demand to be visited in the same trip.

Pisidian · turned Alexander away

Termessos

A defensive city at 1000 m, ringed by Mount Solymos. Alexander called it the eagle's nest and chose not to attack.

MountainNecropolis
 Pisidian · Roman provincial capital

Sagalassos

One of the most spectacularly preserved upland cities in Anatolia, with a reconstructed Antonine nymphaeum still flowing.
